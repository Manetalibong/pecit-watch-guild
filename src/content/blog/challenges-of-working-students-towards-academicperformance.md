<p><strong>ABSTRACT</strong><br>This study endeavors to investigate the challenges encountered by working students<br>and their subsequent influence on academic performance. In the face of escalating<br>educational costs and a growing aspiration for financial autonomy, many students<br>find themselves compelled to navigate the dual demands of work and academics.<br>Employing a quantitative research methodology, this study delves into the difference<br>between the challenges faced by working students and their academic performance,<br>spanning from the first year to the fourth year at the Philippine Electronics and<br>Communication Institute of Technology. The researchers utilized Challenges of the<br>Respondents&#8217; Scale to gauge these variables, employing statistical techniques,<br>including T Statistic and standard deviation. The study&#8217;s findings reveal that a<br>majority of the respondents are female, with a smaller representation of males.<br>Furthermore, it is observed that most working students successfully manage their<br>work commitments alongside their academic responsibilities, suggesting that<br>working while studying may not be a substantial impediment to fulfilling their<br>educational tasks. However, these challenges may have implications for their future<br>careers, given their early exposure to the workforce. In light of these findings, the<br>study concludes with recommendations to address the challenges identified, offering<br>potential strategies to enhance the academic and professional trajectories of<br>working students.</p>
