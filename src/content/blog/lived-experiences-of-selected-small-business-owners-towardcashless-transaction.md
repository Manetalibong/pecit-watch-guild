<p><strong>Keywords</strong>&#8211; Lived experiences, Cashless transaction, Descriptive-phenomenological<br>Design, Philippines</p>



<p>Cashless transactions, the medium in which transactions happen wherein they do not<br>involve actual cash, have become a widespread form of transaction not just at the<br>domestic level but also at the international level. Its continuous progress alters not just<br>the Philippine economy but also the people&#8217;s behavior. This study used a descriptivephenomenological research design to describe the lived experiences of the ten (10)<br>selected small business owners toward cashless transactions. The researchers<br>developed an interview guide as an instrument to determine the participants&#8217; lived<br>experiences, and thematic analysis was used to interpret the data. Thematic data<br>disclosed that adapting cashless transactions resulted in both positive and negative<br>aspects, namely versatility and adaptability, balance discrepancies, and intermittent<br>access. Hence, participants experienced a worthwhile and unpleasant experience<br>upon embracing cashless transactions.</p>
