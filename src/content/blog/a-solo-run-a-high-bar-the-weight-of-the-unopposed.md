<p>The air inside the hall during the Miting de Avance was thick, not just with the heat of the afternoon, but<br>with the weight of promises. As the PECIT student body prepares to head to the polls, we find ourselves<br>at a unique and somewhat unsettling crossroads. <br><br>With the &#8220;Light Partylist&#8221; running as the sole ticket,<br>this election is no longer a battle of choices, but a trial of character. It is no longer about choosing who<br>can lead, but about whether this singular group possesses the proper decorum and substance to assume<br>office.<br>The candidates, presenting themselves in polished attire under the banner of &#8220;Serving with Integrity,<br>Leading with Heart,&#8221; have a clear path to victory—but a rocky path to credibility. As the panel and<br>audience began to peel back the layers of their platforms, the &#8220;heart&#8221; seemed heavy, and the &#8220;integrity&#8221;<br>felt remarkably thin.<br><br>Presidential candidate Princess Cadez banked heavily on a &#8220;Premium Canva for Dean’s Listers&#8221; initiative.<br>On the surface, it’s a shiny reward for academic excellence—a digital carrot dangled in front of the<br>studious. While Cadez assured the crowd that the SSG coffers would bear the brunt of the cost, the<br>proposal feels more like a niche luxury than a systemic solution for the entire student body. Is the SSG a<br>reward dispensary for the few, or a governing body for the many?<br>Perhaps more concerning was the vague call for &#8220;intervention&#8221; to create a harmonious PECIT<br>community. When pressed for concrete programs to bridge the gap between uncooperative students<br>and school spirit, the response was hollow.<br><br>To claim that intervention is necessary without providing a<br>roadmap is like a doctor prescribing &#8220;health&#8221; without a treatment plan. Intervention without action is<br>simply hopeless.<br>The tension broke into unintentional comedy when Vice Presidential candidate Princess Faith Blanco<br>defended the &#8220;Youth Jam.&#8221; What started as a claim of a non-affiliated &#8220;praise and worship&#8221; session<br>quickly unraveled. When asked how this program benefits the diverse student body, Blanco retreated<br>into a shield of personal &#8220;purpose,&#8221; essentially telling the voters that the why was no longer her<br>responsibility to explain.<br><br>Even more alarming was the offer to have her own church facilitate the event for free. <br>While it sounds<br>like a bargain, it reeks of partisanship. The SSG is a secular, student-led institution, not a satellite office<br>for a specific religious organization. Using a position of power to usher in private interests is a dangerous<br>precedent.<br><br>While the President stood her ground, the rest of the slate seemed to crumble under the slightest heat.<br>The Independent candidates reacted with visible anger when questioned about their &#8220;CR Monitoring&#8221;<br>slogan—a basic administrative task being touted as a revolutionary platform. If a candidate reacts to a<br>question as if it were an assault, how will they handle the actual pressures of office?</p>



<p>Make no mistake: this writing piece will be etched into the history of PECIT. As the Light Partylist<br>prepares to take their seats by default, the student body will be watching with a ledger in hand. We are<br>moving from the season of campaigning into the era of accountability.<br>We look forward with healthy skepticism to the feasibility of their cause. In an unopposed election, the<br>&#8220;win&#8221; is easy, but the &#8220;trust&#8221; must be earned. PECIT, do not let the lack of competition lull you into<br>silence. Hold them to the decorum they claim to possess, and hold their promises to the fire. History is<br>watching, and so are we.<br>The win may be guaranteed, but the legacy is yet to be written.</p>
