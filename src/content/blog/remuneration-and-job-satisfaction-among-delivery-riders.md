<p><strong>ABSTRACT</strong><br>Delivery riders are the front-line workers who interacted directly with customers and<br>deliver their orders. These riders do not enjoy the benefits that they should get as<br>regular workers of the food delivery apps that, for all intents and purposes, employed<br>them—no assurances of income, no accident insurance, no social security, and no<br>protection from termination. This quantitative and descriptive-correlational research<br>investigated the relationship between remuneration and job satisfaction among fifty<br>(50) delivery riders in Butuan City, Philippines. The researchers made use of likert<br>scale survey questionnaire to determine the range of remuneration and the factors that<br>affects job satisfaction. The data gathered were analyzed, correlate, and interpret. The<br>analyzed data revealed an average level of commitment, moderate level of work<br>environment, and low level of incentives and benefits. Moreover, there is no significant<br>relationship between the remuneration and the level of job satisfaction among delivery<br>riders. The findings concluded that delivery riders in Butuan City did not have a<br>significant relationship between the delivery riders’ remuneration and overall job<br>satisfaction. This means that within the range observed in the study, variations in<br>income do not notably impact how satisfied riders feel about their work. It was<br>suggested that other factors played a more significant role in delivery rider satisfaction<br>in Butuan City, such as the work environment, incentives, benefits, or commitment.</p>
