<p><strong>Abstract</strong></p>



<p>The study investigated the extent of attributes in selecting the Bachelor of Science in<br>Business Administration program for first-year college students. The study employed<br>a mixed-method, descriptive-correlational design, combining quantitative and<br>qualitative approaches. The study used a survey questionnaire with two distinct parts.<br>The first part gathered information about the respondents&#8217; profiles, while the second<br>part, the survey proper, posed questions to investigate the topic of interest. The<br>questionnaire concluded with a sentence completion task. One hundred first- year<br>BSBA students participated in the study, chosen due to their program&#8217;s high<br>enrollment. Convenient sampling involved readily available students completing the<br>questionnaire. The researchers used a scoring procedure and descriptivecorrelational to measure the two variables. Findings revealed a diverse student body<br>driven by individual motivations and backgrounds rather than demographics. Young<br>students (17-19 years old) from lower-middle-income families seeking business<br>careers were most attracted to BSBA. Despite varied personal goals, including<br>entrepreneurship and leadership aspirations, these students shared a strong work<br>ethic and sought the program&#8217;s tools to navigate the business world. This diversity<br>underscores BSBA&#8217;s potential to cultivate future leaders and entrepreneurs across<br>varied demographics. Based on their findings, the researchers recommend focusing<br>BSBA recruitment efforts on young women from lower-middle-income families.<br>Highlighting the importance of family support and career/interest alignment is critical<br>in attracting these students.</p>
