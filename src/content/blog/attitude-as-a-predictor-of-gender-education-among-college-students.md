<p><strong>ABSTRACT</strong></p>



<p><em>It is fascinating to consider how deeply ingrained </em><em>the</em><em> initial thoughts and feelings about gender might influence our journey through gender education in college.</em><em> This study explored the relationship between gender education and students&#8217; attitudes and behaviors related to gender concepts. Data analysis from various tables revealed that gender education positively impacts students&#8217; views on gender equality and their willingness to challenge gender stereotypes. The data showed a strong correlation between educational programs and support for gender equality, with students receiving more education demonstrating more progressive views. Moreover, the impact on core beliefs about gender roles seems less pronounced; the study suggests a nudge towards less traditional views. Interestingly, a potential link between a student&#8217;s primary and willingness to address the gender gap emerged, with students in social sciences and humanities showing a slight positive correlation. Overall, the study concludes that gender education plays a significant role in shaping positive attitudes and potentially influencing actions that promote gender equality. Further research is recommended to explore the </em><em>distinction</em><em> of th</em><em>e</em><em> relationship and investigate areas with less clear connections, such as the link between education and empowerment related to gender roles.</em></p>
